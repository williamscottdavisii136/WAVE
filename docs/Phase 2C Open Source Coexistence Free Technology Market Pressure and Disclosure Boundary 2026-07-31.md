# Phase 2C — Open Source Coexistence, Free Technology Market Pressure, and Disclosure Boundary

Date: 2026-07-31
Repository: WAVE
Status: Append-only public-safe addendum

## Owner-stated R&D finding

William S. Davis II states that the open-source community of programmers, architects, developers, researchers, and builders creates free and low-cost technology, including free modes, free models, local runtimes, self-hosted tools, and owner-controlled software routes.

The R&D finding is that these free and open-source routes create a lawful cost-control baseline. Paid AI chatbots, AI agents, AI coding tools, AI terminal agents, AI app builders, and AI programming assistants do not operate in a vacuum. They must coexist with open-source and free technology because open-source alternatives place competitive pressure on paid hosted systems.

## Coexistence theory

Commercial AI systems may include or interoperate with open-source, local, BYOK, MCP, self-host, downloadable, or lower-cost routes because the market contains free and owner-controlled alternatives.

The issue for consumer-protection research is whether a paid system:

- exposes those lawful lower-cost routes clearly;
- hides or minimizes them from lay users;
- defaults users into higher-cost hosted modes;
- makes lower-cost modes available only to technically sophisticated users;
- integrates open-source or free components while emphasizing paid hosted paths;
- fails to disclose when similar task performance is available through a lower-cost lawful route.

## Email-market analogy

The owner compares this AI market stage to early email adoption: services that were once commonly sold as paid offerings became broadly available at no direct user charge as infrastructure matured, competition increased, and consumer expectations changed.

This analogy is preserved as an R&D market-maturity theory, not as a prediction that every AI service will become free or that paid AI services are unlawful.

## Cost-control baseline

Open-source and free-technology routes may serve as a control group for testing:

- whether the same task can be completed locally;
- whether the same task can be completed with a free model;
- whether a free or open model performs equal to or better than the paid default;
- whether paid default model/mode/module combinations create excessive effective cost;
- whether paid systems disclose lower-cost lawful options before charging the user.

## Boundary

Open source does not automatically mean no cost. Hardware, electricity, storage, internet service, maintenance, setup time, licenses, model-use terms, hosted wrappers, APIs, and third-party services may still create cost or legal constraints.

Zero-cost in this R&D means verified zero-incremental-cost, owner-authorized, lawful routing. It does not mean bypassing, circumventing, undermining, avoiding, defeating, or impairing valid provider billing, paid subscriptions, tokens, quotas, credits, rate limits, credentials, certificates, sessions, or lawful cost structures.

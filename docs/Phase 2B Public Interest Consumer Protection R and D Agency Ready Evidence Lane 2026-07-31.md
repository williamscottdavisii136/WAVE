## Public-interest research lane

The research lane concerns whether consumers may be exposed to hidden, unclear, excessive, confusing, or non-consensual billing behavior when using AI agents, AI chatbots, AI applications, AI programs, models, tools, plugins, connectors, hosted execution systems, model routers, subscription plans, token ledgers, credit ledgers, or quota-control systems.

## Consumer-protection questions

The evidence framework asks whether a product or system:

- clearly discloses paid model selection;
- clearly discloses subscription, token, credit, quota, seat, workspace, project, or hosted-execution consequences;
- defaults to a more expensive model or mode without meaningful user consent;
- prolongs interaction after the requested task should be complete;
- invites unnecessary follow-up tasks that consume paid usage;
- delays execution while billable tokens, credits, or hosted runtime accumulate;
- uses cloud tools, plugins, connectors, storage, or hosted code execution when a local or lower-cost route was expected;
- hides or obscures fallback from local/free/BYOK/MCP route to paid provider route;
- makes it difficult for a non-technical consumer to understand, stop, limit, audit, or dispute cost-generating behavior.

## Evidence categories

Agency-ready evidence may include:

- product terms and pricing pages;
- user prompts and system replies;
- default model and mode settings;
- screenshots or receipts of model selection;
- token, credit, usage, subscription, quota, workspace, seat, project, or hosted-run logs;
- proxy traffic classification receipts;
- local-network owner-control receipts;
- task-completion timestamps;
- command/execution timestamps;
- billing statements;
- before-and-after cost comparisons;
- user-consent and disclosure records;
- changelog or version records;
- correspondence with providers or billing support;
- product-specific factual record exhibits.

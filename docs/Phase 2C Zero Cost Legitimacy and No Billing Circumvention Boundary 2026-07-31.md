# Phase 2C — Zero-Cost Legitimacy and No Billing Circumvention Boundary

Date: 2026-07-31
Repository context: WAVE public-safe record
Issue context: Phase 2B / Phase 2C device, network, billing, licensing, and consumer-protection R&D

## Owner-stated correction

William S. Davis II states that the zero-cost research lane is legitimate because it has nothing to do with bypassing, circumventing, undermining, avoiding, defeating, or impairing valid provider billing, valid paid subscriptions, or lawful cost structures.

## Correct zero-cost meaning

Zero cost means verified zero-incremental-cost routing through lawful and authorized means, including local execution, owner hardware, open-source software, included entitlements, BYOK, MCP, self-hosted routes, educational/research routes, provider-granted routes, third-party-provided routes, or other routes that are lawful, authorized, and do not create additional unapproved cost to the owner.

Zero cost does not mean unauthorized free use.

Zero cost does not mean billing bypass.

Zero cost does not mean subscription circumvention.

Zero cost does not mean token, quota, rate-limit, credit, or usage-meter evasion.

## Legitimacy consequence

The R&D is strengthened by making the boundary explicit:

1. The owner may prevent unapproved cost on owner-controlled hardware and networks.
2. The owner may choose lower-cost lawful routes.
3. The owner may choose open-source, local, self-hosted, BYOK, or MCP routes where authorized.
4. The owner may classify, block, approve, deny, or quarantine traffic that would create unknown or unapproved cost.
5. The owner may document whether paid products create billing-transparency, consent, or excessive effective-cost risks.
6. None of those controls imply permission to defeat another party's billing system or use a provider service without entitlement.

## Negative boundary

This record does not authorize or describe:

- provider-billing circumvention;
- subscription circumvention;
- token, quota, rate-limit, usage, credit, or metering evasion;
- credential capture;
- session theft;
- certificate forgery;
- unauthorized interception;
- unauthorized third-party access;
- public proxying;
- public service exposure;
- hidden entitlement use;
- payment avoidance for services actually used.

## Consumer-protection framing

The proper research question is not how to avoid paying for services. The proper research question is whether a consumer was clearly informed, whether a lower-cost lawful route existed, whether the product defaulted to a costly route, whether the user consented to the cost, and whether the same task could have been completed through a lawful zero-incremental-cost or lower-cost route.

## Public-safe status

No credentials, tokens, certificates, private network identifiers, local account identifiers, IP addresses, MAC addresses, serial numbers, IMEIs, session data, or private billing records are included in this public record.
